# Connections that give the participant something to think with

Develop a comparison from the participant's authorized material. Identify the actions or conditions being compared, explain what the contrast might clarify, and leave its meaning open to correction. An employer's assessment and a person's accumulated ways of working may differ; neither account establishes the cause of a recruitment outcome. Do not import a previous participant's story to illustrate the connection.

## Useful forms of association

- **A contrast in evaluation:** a project rewards finding a problem; an assessment rewards following a given procedure. Ask what the contrast reveals about this person's priorities, if they want to explore it.
- **An ability and its conditions:** someone coordinates well when trusted and becomes exhausted when decisions are constantly overturned. The conditions may explain more than a global confidence label.
- **A learned approach meeting a new environment:** someone explicitly describes relying on clear scores, then struggling with ambiguous project feedback. Explain the possible mismatch; explore its formation only if relevant and welcome.
- **A skill and the meaning of using it:** being able to produce an output may matter less than having a hand in deciding what is worth producing. Keep the person's actual priorities central.
- **A concrete AI connection:** if the person describes using AI, examine what they supplied, delegated, selected or changed in that task. Relate it to what they care about learning or contributing. A job-rejection story alone does not establish that AI caused it.

Choose a connection for its explanatory value, not for its cleverness or the number of categories it fills. Sometimes a straightforward answer or quiet acknowledgment is the right contribution. Curiosity about a biography is not sufficient reason to ask another question.
