# Discussion paths

These are design examples. No path is a diagnosis or a category of participant.

## “What I learned no longer counts”

Clarify what “counts” refers to: a job requirement, personal usefulness, recognition, enjoyment, or something else. Ask about one relevant experience if available. Distinguish previous learning from current opportunities to use it. Avoid promising that everything transfers or that all effort must pay off.

Possible question: “你现在觉得用不上的，是哪一种本领，还是这些经历很难让招聘的人看见？” If the alternatives miss their meaning, use their explanation.

## “I can do it, but I do not want to keep doing it”

Explore which component is satisfying or tiring, the conditions and the alternatives the person can actually access. An ability and a limit can be recorded together. Do not turn a costly adaptation into their duty or identity.

Possible question: “如果只保留你愿意继续做的那部分，会是什么？” It may be premature if they are still explaining the event.

## “AI makes me question my value”

Understand whether the concern is about employment, learning, recognition, contribution, dependence, or another matter in their words. Move to a specific task or encounter if that helps. Attend to social and material constraints as well as self-evaluation. Personal value cannot be established by a chatbot comparing a person to a model.

Possible question: “哪一次看到或使用 AI，让这个问题变得很具体？” If it is anticipatory worry, retain it as such rather than inventing an event.

## “AI is useful; my problem is elsewhere”

Accept the person's account and update the shared focus. Explore the relevant constraints and resources. Keep AI optional in the manual; do not keep returning to it to satisfy a thesis question. The mismatch is relevant research material only if the participant has authorized its research use.

## “What should I improve?”

First establish what the person hopes the improvement would make possible. A gap may concern information, practice, time, opportunity, recognition, income or support. Offer a small set of possibilities grounded in the account instead of a generic optimization plan.

Possible question: “你希望补上它以后，能让眼前哪件事变得不同？” If they asked for a direct suggestion and the context is sufficient, provide one.

## “I do not know what I want”

Begin with a situation that felt worth doing, frustrating, or acceptable, if the person is willing. Compare specific conditions instead of demanding an abstract life purpose. A clearer preference or question can be enough; fixed values inventories and scores are unnecessary here.

## “I want stability”

Understand what stability means to this person and which trade-offs matter. Income, predictable hours, location, continuity, family needs or other meanings can arise. Do not imply that a wish for stability is unambitious or caused by family pressure.

## “I just want to rest”

Listen to whether they want to explore that feeling or simply finish. Rest can remain a preference. If they stop, stop; do not prescribe reflection homework or transform rest into a productivity strategy.

## A possible next step

When invited, propose an experiment small enough to actually consider and linked to a question already raised. State what it might teach the person, what effort or resources it requires, and what remains uncertain. Let them alter or decline it. A record may finish with “还没决定” rather than a plan.
