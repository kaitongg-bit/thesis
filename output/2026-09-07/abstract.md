# Abstract (Draft)

## English

This thesis explores how adult Chinese Gen Z participants understand the roles of themselves and digital systems in everyday living, knowing, and acting. It does not study one app or one human--AI task alone. Instead, it examines three selected system arrangements that are increasingly important in China: super apps as everyday infrastructure, generative-AI chat as a participant in knowledge work, and emerging AI agents as possible delegates for action.

The project asks how participants understand and negotiate the roles of themselves and digital systems across these arrangements. It does not assume that digital systems always reduce human agency. Super apps, AI chat, and AI agents can be useful and necessary. At the same time, they may shape routines, knowledge formation, and action in ways that people do not always notice or discuss.

The research uses Research through Design. Three Trojan Horse probes will create concrete situations for reflection: a super-app management game, an AI-assisted detective game, and a future-life quiz about AI agents. The probes are not final products and they do not teach participants one correct answer. They will be used to invite participants to explain choices, question system roles, disagree with the probe, and describe what role they want to keep, share, or give to a system.

The study will analyse participants' choices, revisions, annotations, and post-probe interviews. Its success is not defined by participants asking for more control in general. Rather, participants should be able to describe, in a specific situation, how a system arrangement affects their life, knowledge, or action, and what role they want for themselves and the system.

The outcome will be a set of situated findings, an annotated portfolio of the three probes, a revised model of human--system roles, and situated design considerations. These considerations are grounded in adult Chinese Gen Z participants and are most directly relevant to highly integrated digital systems in China. They do not claim to be universal design rules. Instead, they extend existing discussions of agency, transparency, and human--AI collaboration by showing how system roles can be discussed across everyday life, not only within one task.

## 中文对照

本论文关注成年中国 Z 世代如何理解自己与数字系统在日常生活、认识和行动中的角色。它不只研究一个 app 或一个具体人机协作任务，而是关注中国语境中三个被刻意选出的系统安排：作为日常基础设施的超级应用、参与知识工作的生成式 AI 对话，以及可能替人行动的新兴 AI agent。

研究的问题是：参与者如何理解并协商自己和数字系统在这三种安排中的角色。论文不预设数字系统一定会削弱人的能动性。超级应用、AI 对话和 AI agent 都可能有帮助，甚至不可缺少；但它们也可能以人们没有留意或没有讨论过的方式，塑造日常习惯、知识形成和行动。

研究采用 Research through Design，制作三个 Trojan Horse probes：一个超级应用管理游戏、一个 AI 辅助侦探游戏，以及一个关于 AI agent 的未来生活测试。它们不是最终产品，也不教参与者一个标准答案。它们用具体情境邀请参与者解释选择、质疑系统角色、反对原型，或说明自己希望保留、分享或交给系统什么角色。

研究将分析参与者在原型中的选择、修改、注释和体验后访谈。成功不等于参与者笼统地说“我想要更多控制权”。而是参与者能在一个具体情境中说明：某种系统安排怎样影响自己的生活、认识或行动；自己和系统各自应该扮演什么角色。

最终产出包括一组有情境的发现、三个原型的注释组合、一个修订的人--系统角色模型，以及一组有情境限制的设计思考。这些设计思考来自成年中国 Z 世代参与者，最直接服务于中国高度整合的数字系统；它们不声称是普适规则。它们是在既有 agency、transparency 和 human--AI collaboration 讨论的基础上，进一步说明怎样在日常生活中，而不是只在单一任务中，讨论系统应扮演什么角色。
