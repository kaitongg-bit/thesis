# Weekly Assignment: Research Question, Canvas Post, and Thesis Outline

## 1. Research question developed through the three steps

### Step 1: Topic — I am studying...

I am studying how adult Chinese Gen Z understand their own role and the role of digital systems when super apps, generative-AI chat, and emerging AI agents take part in everyday living, knowing, and acting.

### Step 2: Question — because I want to find out...

I want to find out how participants describe what they want to do themselves, what they are willing to share with a system, and what they are willing to delegate in different situations.

### Step 3: Significance — in order to help my audience understand...

I want to help design researchers and practitioners understand human-system roles beyond one interface feature or one task. I also want to show how these roles are shaped by the Chinese context, where highly integrated platforms already organise many parts of everyday life.

### Main research question

**How do adult Chinese Gen Z understand and negotiate their own role, and the role of digital systems, across everyday living, knowing, and acting in the context of super apps, generative-AI chat, and emerging AI agents?**

## 2. Canvas discussion post

### Topic, question, motivation, and possible contribution

My research is about the roles that people and digital systems take in everyday life. I focus on adult Chinese Gen Z and three selected types of systems: super apps, generative-AI chat, and emerging AI agents. I chose these cases because they show three different ways that a digital system can become involved in life. A super app can become infrastructure for communication, payment, shopping, and services. AI chat can take part in searching, writing, and forming ideas. An AI agent may act for a person in situations that include personal values, relationships, and changing circumstances.

**How do adult Chinese Gen Z understand and negotiate their own role, and the role of digital systems, across everyday living, knowing, and acting in the context of super apps, generative-AI chat, and emerging AI agents?**

This question matters because digital systems are often discussed as tools that help users complete tasks. Many human-AI studies examine a clear activity, such as drawing, writing, or making a decision with AI. Other work offers important principles such as transparency, correction, explanation, and user control. My project builds on this research, but asks a different level of question. Before deciding how to improve one task, how do people understand what role a system should have in their lives, and what role they want to keep for themselves?

The Chinese context is important to this question. Platforms such as WeChat are not only individual products. They connect social life, payment, information, services, and institutions. This makes China a useful context for studying what happens when a digital system becomes part of the conditions of everyday life. However, I will not claim to represent all Chinese Gen Z or every part of their lives. The three system arrangements give the project clear boundaries.

I will use Research through Design to explore the question. I plan to create three Trojan Horse design probes: a super-app management game, an AI-assisted detective game, and a future-life quiz about AI agents. The probes will create concrete situations in which participants can explain, question, defend, or change the roles they give to themselves and the system. They are research instruments, not final products and not lessons that tell participants what to think.

The main audience includes interaction designers, HCI and design researchers, and people designing integrated platforms or AI systems in China. The outcome will not be a new universal rule for all users. I expect to produce situated findings, an annotated portfolio of the probes, a revised model of human-system roles, and design considerations grounded in the experiences of adult Chinese Gen Z. These outcomes may help designers discuss human-system relationships across everyday life, not only the efficiency of one task.

## 3. Thesis outline

### 1. Introduction: the research problem

- Describe how super apps, AI chat, and AI agents take different roles in everyday life.
- Introduce the Chinese context without treating all Chinese Gen Z as one group.
- Explain the gap between studying one human-AI task and studying system roles across everyday life.
- Present the main research question and the three bounded cases.

### 2. Literature and conceptual background

#### 2.1 Digital platforms as everyday infrastructure in China

- WeChat and super apps as both platforms and infrastructure.
- Integration of communication, payment, services, information, and institutions.
- Limited alternatives, social expectations, and practical dependence.

#### 2.2 Human agency and human-system roles

- Agency as forming goals, understanding a process, judging results, acting, and taking responsibility.
- Existing work on transparency, explanation, correction, control, and meaningful human control.
- The difference between a control feature and a person's wider role in a system relationship.

#### 2.3 AI chat and ways of knowing

- AI participation in searching, writing, interpretation, and judgement.
- Questions of contribution, verification, dependence, and knowledge ownership.

#### 2.4 AI agents and delegated action

- The difference between giving advice and acting for a person.
- Rules, exceptions, relationships, changing contexts, and responsibility.

#### 2.5 Research through Design and design probes

- Prototypes as ways to produce situations, questions, and knowledge.
- Reflective and provocative design.
- Why probes can reveal experiences that are difficult to express through abstract interview questions alone.

### 3. Research design

- Participants: adult Chinese Gen Z, with the final age or birth-year definition stated clearly.
- Probe 1: *Evil PM Simulator* — super apps as infrastructure for living.
- Probe 2: *Epistemic Illusion* — AI chat as a participant in knowing.
- Probe 3: *Cyber-Utopia Persona Quiz* — AI agents as delegates for acting.
- Data: choices inside the probes, changes to earlier choices, short annotations, observation, and post-probe interviews.
- Analysis: thematic analysis within each probe, followed by comparison across the three probes.
- Research stance: record agreement, disagreement, mixed feelings, and counterexamples.

### 4. Findings

- How participants describe the system's role and their own role in each probe.
- When systems feel helpful, necessary, shaping, difficult to refuse, or inappropriate.
- What participants want to keep, share, or delegate.
- Differences and tensions across living, knowing, and acting.
- Findings that challenge the initial Reference Model.

### 5. Discussion

- A revised account of human-system roles across the three system arrangements.
- What is specific to the Chinese context and what may be relevant elsewhere.
- How the findings relate to existing work on agency, transparency, control, and human-AI collaboration.
- Situated design considerations rather than universal design rules.
- Limits: small qualitative sample, designed probe situations, and no claim to represent all Chinese Gen Z.

### 6. Contributions and conclusion

- Situated findings from adult Chinese Gen Z participants.
- An annotated portfolio of three connected design probes.
- A revised model of system roles across living, knowing, and acting.
- Design considerations for discussing and designing human-system roles in highly integrated digital contexts.
- Questions for later research and comparison with other social or cultural contexts.

## 4. Focused reading plan

### Read first: directly shapes the thesis

1. Plantin, J.-C., & de Seta, G. (2019). *WeChat as infrastructure: The techno-nationalist shaping of Chinese digital platforms.*  
   Use it to explain why WeChat is more than an app and why the Chinese context matters.

2. Amershi, S., et al. (2019). *Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction.*  
   Use it to show what existing human-AI design principles already cover, including explanation, correction, feedback, and control. This helps clarify that the thesis is not claiming to invent these ideas.

3. Siebert, L. C., et al. (2022). *Meaningful human control: Actionable properties for AI system development.*  
   Use it to distinguish responsibility and meaningful control from simply adding more interface buttons.

4. Zimmerman, J., Forlizzi, J., & Evenson, S. (2007). *Research through Design as a Method for Interaction Design Research in HCI.*  
   Use it to explain how designed artefacts can carry and produce research knowledge.

5. Gaver, W. (2012). *What Should We Expect from Research through Design?*  
   Use it to frame the probes as an annotated portfolio and the findings as situated, provisional knowledge rather than universal theory.

### Read next: strengthens method and interpretation

6. Boehner, K., Vertesi, J., Sengers, P., & Dourish, P. (2007). *How HCI Interprets the Probes.*  
   Use it to avoid treating probe responses as simple objective data and to explain the interpretive role of the researcher.

7. Escobar, A. (2018). *Designs for the Pluriverse.*  
   Use it to explain why design shapes relationships, practices, and possible ways of living, rather than only producing neutral tools.

8. Booth, W. C., Colomb, G. G., Williams, J. M., et al. (2024). *The Craft of Research* (5th ed.).  
   Use the topic-question-significance structure to keep testing the research question and its relevance to an audience.

## 5. Working definition to keep the scope clear

This thesis does not study all parts of Chinese Gen Z life. It studies one broad issue through three bounded system arrangements. Its unit of inquiry is how participants describe the roles of people and systems in concrete situations. “Living, knowing, and acting” are analytical categories used to compare the three probes; they are not a claim to cover the whole of human life.
